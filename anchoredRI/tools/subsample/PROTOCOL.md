# Task H. Subsampling large routine datasets

**Proposed for review, 26 November 2026.** At N = 50,000, full-data anchoredRI Arm B selected four or five mixture components on 1,300 of 1,440 RIbench datasets (90.3%). Its first-stage safeguards declined 305 (21.2%). Reducing the routine sample from nominal 50,000 to 5,000 gives the same 40 anchors about ten times their relative weight in the likelihood. BIC may then select fewer components, and the anchors may have more influence on the healthy component’s limits. We will test whether taking the median of 50 such fits improves the limits, and whether giving all routine observations a combined weight of 5,000 achieves a similar result. RIbench informed this idea. Adoption would require independent confirmation data and a prespecified rule for which sample sizes between 5,000 and 50,000 should use the method. [Task E component counts and decline rates](https://github.com/amromeo/amromeo.github.io/blob/8bcd49c3c7977c50982524b9dbc48d16bdfa5914/anchoredRI/tools/boxcox/accuracy/large/results/REPORT.md)

The archived component-share analysis is complete. New fits, the timing pilot and the full benchmark await approval. Study files belong in `anchoredRI/tools/subsample/` on `study/subsample`. [Archived component-share report](archived-shares/REPORT.md)

## Arm W. One weighted fit to the full routine vector

Fit the current anchoredRI mixture to all observed routine values with constant weight $q=5000/n$ and each of the 40 anchors with weight 1. On the original measurement scale, maximize

$$
\ell_W = q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k f_k(x_i)\right)
       +\sum_{j=1}^{40}\log f_1(a_j).
$$

Here $f_k$ includes the transform Jacobian and component 1 is the screened healthy component. Anchors contribute directly to component 1. Apply the same routine weights to the likelihood, Jacobian and derivatives, including at tied values. Fractional counts retain double precision. Keep the full-data rounding grid, search geometry, component constraints, eligible transformations and multistart procedure. Use the weighted joint mean and variance for the analytic one-component start. Use $N_{\mathrm{BIC}}=\sum_i q_i+40$, the total weight of the routine observations and anchors. For W this is 5,040, the same sample size used by an ordinary subsample fit. Rank candidates on the **summed** original-scale likelihood using $-2\ell_W+p\log(N_{\mathrm{BIC}})$, where $p=2K+(K-1)$ for one routine setting, plus one parameter when fitting a log shift. If the optimizer uses a mean objective, divide its summed objective and gradient by the same total weight. The safeguards use the observed full-data minimum and the original 40 anchors.

Return W's raw component-1 limits and its own two-check decision. W is a single weighted mixture fit. Also show the same W estimate under the two shared release rules below, labeled as alternative decisions, so differences in withholding can be separated from differences in the estimated limits. W's own safeguards and its raw endpoints stay visible in every row.

## Safeguards and release rules

On the full mixture, each subsample mixture and W, run both current anchoredRI checks. On the selected transform and shift, compare the Gaussian likelihood of the 40 anchors under their own maximum-likelihood mean and SD with that under fitted component 1. Use the package's nominal two-degree-of-freedom likelihood-ratio test and flag strictly at $p<0.01$. Independently flag a two-sided lower limit **strictly below the minimum routine value used in that fit**. Each subsample has its own observed minimum. CRP skips the lower-limit check. Record both flags, their intersection, p-value, minimum and technical errors. Either statistical flag declines one fit and a fit can cast only one vote. [Safeguard source](https://github.com/amromeo/amromeo.github.io/blob/fe8105ac6961f1b45bbfbe034648938f227c4726/anchoredRI/R/safeguard.R#L37-L73)

| Release rule for the same median | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Full-data safeguard | Release when the unweighted full fit passes. Decline when that fit declines. An unavailable full fit or check gives a technical withholding. |
| Subsample majority | Decline when at least **26 of the 50** subsamples decline. A 25–25 split releases the estimate. Use all 50 attempted draws as the denominator. |

For the majority rule, if $v$ draws decline and $u$ are technically unresolved, decline if $v \ge 26$, release if $v+u \le 25$, and otherwise record technical withholding. A missing aggregate interval is always a technical nonreturn. Use the subsamples’ first-stage statuses for both median definitions. Take each saved full-package status and any fallback reason directly from the full-data record. Report a cross-tabulation of the two safeguard rules, including which rule released each discordant case.
