Fresh datasets

Does the improvement repeat?

Download summary

Each row uses 20 newly generated datasets with 5,000 routine patients. The two versions receive the same patients and screened results. The design varies the offset, skew, disease overlap and screened sample size. These runs compare anchoredRI 0.1 with automatic selection in 0.2; refineR was not rerun in this repeated test.

For the scenario matching the fourth example, mean endpoint error fell from 7.12% to 1.04%. All three unshifted scenarios were unchanged. In the shifted scenario with strong disease overlap, error increased from 8.51% to 11.83% on average. All 440 fits returned estimates; numerical success did not prevent that bias.

MPE is averaged across both endpoints and all successful paired datasets. Smaller is better. The win counts compare errors within each pair; ties differ by no more than 0.000001 percentage point. The CSV also reports the Monte Carlo standard error of the paired change, failures, boundary fits and selected families. Twenty datasets per scenario give a preliminary estimate of performance. These point-estimate runs do not measure confidence-interval coverage.

What follows the authors’ guidance

A suitable input population

Each fit uses one physiological partition from two eligible services. Expected affected fractions are 10% or 25%. Both methods use the same routine patients. In a clinical study, assay comparability, age/sex partitions, repeated patients and high-risk services would need actual review.

The released refineR workflow

refineR 2.0.0 runs 200 bootstrap resamples and reports medianBS estimates with 95% CIs. The normal and lognormal examples use BoxCox. The shifted example uses modBoxCox, and its default fit is available for inspection.

Direct estimates from 120 and 400

These rows use the empirical 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles, as in the original paper’s direct-method comparison. The earlier stress page retains its Gaussian mean-and-SD estimates. The 40-, 120- and 400-person reference samples are nested and independent of routine patients.

Conditions that challenge each method

Overlapping disease can bias refineR even when most results are non-pathological. The shifted lognormal example exposes a limitation of anchoredRI 0.1. Version 0.2 estimates the offset jointly with the mixture. It receives no true distribution parameters. Screened labels are perfect in all examples.

Automatic selection compares normal, lognormal and shifted-lognormal mixtures with one to three components. BIC scores use the original measurement scale and penalize the extra offset parameter. Every bootstrap resamples both services and screened patients, then repeats the entire selection and offset fit. Numerical bounds and model assumptions still matter; the shifted-log model does not estimate a general Box-Cox power.
