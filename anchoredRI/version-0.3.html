Version 0.3.0 update

Version 0.3.0 introduced an improved fitter as the default. It compares models with one to five components and checks the selected reference interval before reporting it.

When the package declines an interval

A likelihood-ratio test (LRT) asks whether the fitted reference group agrees with the independently screened patients. A p-value below 0.01 triggers a decline. For a two-sided interval, a lower limit below the smallest routine result also triggers a decline. One-sided requests use the LRT alone.

In version 0.3, a decline left the reported interval missing. The fit gives the reason and keeps both the joint estimate and the screened-only estimate for inspection. Obtain at least 120 screened patients for a direct interval, or verify a suitable published interval with 20 screened patients. Safeguard definitions

What routine results added to 40 screened patients

A separate comparison compared the original anchoredRI 0.2 fits with a model that used the same 40 screened patients alone. The screened-only model could also choose its best-fitting distribution. Adding routine results improved accuracy by the study’s prespecified rule in 28 of 30 scenarios with separated or moderately overlapping reference and disease distributions. Under heavy overlap, the screened-only estimate had lower mean error in all 12 scenarios where 30% to 90% of patients had disease. Screened-patients comparison (Step 1)

RIbench accuracy by dataset size

With 40 independent screened patients, anchoredRI had the lowest error among the methods tested at 1,000 and 5,000 routine results. At 50,000, refineR was more accurate.

Where the method falls short

If many patients have disease and their results overlap heavily with the reference group, an inaccurate interval can pass both checks. Above 5,000 routine results, the only size tested with anchoredRI was 50,000. The study compared the new fitter directly with refineR. refineR on CRAN
