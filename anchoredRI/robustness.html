Package update · November 24, 2026

Reference intervals with anchoredRI 0.3.0

The improved Task E fitter (Arm B) is now the default. It compares models with one to five components and checks the selected reference interval before reporting it.

When the package declines an interval

A likelihood-ratio test (LRT) asks whether the fitted reference group agrees with the independently screened patients. A p-value below 0.01 triggers a decline. For a two-sided interval, a lower limit below the smallest routine result also triggers a decline. One-sided requests use the LRT alone.

A decline means there is no interval to report. The fit gives the reason and keeps both the joint estimate and the screened-only estimate for inspection. Obtain at least 120 screened patients for a direct interval, or verify a suitable published interval with 20 screened patients. Safeguard definitions

What routine results added to 40 screened patients

Step 1 compared the original anchoredRI 0.2 fits with a model fitted to the same 40 screened patients alone. The screened-only model could also choose its best-fitting distribution. Adding routine results improved accuracy by the study’s prespecified rule in 28 of 30 scenarios with separated or moderately overlapping reference and disease distributions. Under heavy overlap, the screened-only estimate had lower mean error in all 12 scenarios with 30% to 90% affected. Step 1 results

Where the method falls short

If many patients are affected and their results overlap heavily with the reference group, an inaccurate interval can pass both checks. Above 5,000 routine results, the only size tested with Arm B was 50,000. For datasets this large, use refineR. anchorRefine, which adds screened patients to refineR, is still in development. Task E compared anchoredRI with refineR directly; anchorRefine was not part of that comparison. refineR on CRAN · anchorRefine repository (work in progress)

What do healthy anchors add?

Across 900 matched datasets, healthy anchors substantially improve mixture fitting. Modeling services separately adds little to overall mean accuracy: 14.06% error for anchoredRI versus 14.09% for the pooled anchored mixture. Heavy overlap remains a limitation.

Completed comparison: 45 scenarios × 20 datasets, with all methods evaluated on the same samples. anchoredRI had lower error than refineR in 693 of 900 datasets (77%). This compares full-data point estimates; the earlier 45 bootstrap examples remain available below.

What was tested

Each dataset has 5,000 routine patients and 40 independent, perfectly labeled healthy anchors. Two simulated services have affected fractions five percentage points below and above the overall fraction. Their labels enter anchoredRI; refineR receives the pooled measurements. The screened-only samples of 40, 120 and 400 people are nested.

The normal, lognormal and shifted-lognormal mixtures are within anchoredRI’s model family. This tests prevalence and overlap, not model misspecification, imperfect screening or differences in healthy physiology across services. The proposed smaller routine samples have not yet been tested.

What the earlier bootstrap study shows

In the matched examples, 32 of 45 refineR estimates and 9 of 45 anchoredRI estimates exceeded the exploratory 10% error threshold. Every method returned finite limits. Of 9,000 requested bootstrap fits per mixture method, refineR had 5 failures and anchoredRI had none. Numerical success does not imply an accurate interval.

At 70% affected with clearly separated normal populations, the true healthy interval was 24.12–35.88. refineR returned 35.35–49.09, close to the disease population’s interval, while anchoredRI returned 24.23–35.96. This breaks refineR’s majority-healthy assumption and illustrates the value of additional healthy labels.

Heavy overlap is a different problem. Across 20 shifted-lognormal datasets with 50% affected and heavy overlap, anchoredRI’s mean error was 51.4%, compared with 18.0% for the same 40 screened patients alone. Its single matched example had only 2.3% error. That discrepancy is why the repeated results and single examples are kept separate.

The new pooled-mixture comparison isolates service grouping within this design: services differ by only ten percentage points in affected fraction, with shared component distributions. Its small overall benefit here does not settle performance with larger service differences. The estimator was frozen and was not tuned to these results. This follow-up reuses the original simulation grid; independent validation, confidence-interval coverage and clinical data remain necessary.
