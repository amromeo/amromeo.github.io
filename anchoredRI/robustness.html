Version 0.3.0 update

Across 900 matched datasets, screened patients substantially improve mixture fitting. Modeling settings separately adds little to overall mean accuracy: 14.06% error for anchoredRI versus 14.09% for the pooled mixture with screened patients. Heavy overlap remains a limitation.

Completed comparison: 45 scenarios × 20 datasets, with all methods evaluated on the same samples. anchoredRI had lower error than refineR in 693 of 900 datasets (77%). This compares full-data point estimates; the earlier 45 bootstrap examples remain available below.

November 24, 2026 · Results by share of patients with disease. In this original study, anchoredRI had lower error than refineR in 121 of 180 datasets (67.2%) where 20% of patients or fewer had disease and in 572 of 720 (79.4%) where more than 20% of patients had disease. The lower group contained only the 10% scenarios. These are the two groups behind the 77% overall figure. Paired results by scenario

What was tested

Each dataset has 5,000 routine patients and 40 independently drawn screened patients known to meet the reference criteria. The two simulated settings have shares of patients with disease five percentage points below and above the overall share. Their labels enter anchoredRI; refineR receives the pooled measurements. The screened-only samples of 40, 120 and 400 people are nested.

The normal, lognormal and shifted-lognormal mixtures are within anchoredRI’s model family. The design varies the share of patients with disease and the overlap between distributions. It assumes perfect screening and the same reference distribution in both settings.

The mixture without screened patients selects the largest component as healthy. The lowest-component sensitivity selects the component with the lowest interval midpoint, a rule favored by this simulation’s upward disease shifts. All three mixture approaches use comparable transformation choices, component counts and fitting procedures. The pooled comparators are study-only versions of the same likelihood, rather than an independent software-package benchmark.

anchoredRI’s overall mean error (14.06%) was slightly higher than that from 40 screened individuals alone (13.60%), although it performed better in 617 of 900 datasets. Large errors in difficult scenarios affect the mean. Service labels reduced the count above 10% from 304 to 289, but barely changed mean error; this does not establish superiority over a pooled mixture with screened patients.

What the earlier bootstrap study shows

In the matched examples, 32 of 45 refineR estimates and 9 of 45 anchoredRI estimates exceeded the exploratory 10% error threshold. Every method returned finite limits. Of 9,000 requested bootstrap fits per mixture method, refineR had 5 failures and anchoredRI had none. Numerical success does not imply an accurate interval.

When 70% of patients had disease with clearly separated normal populations, the true healthy interval was 24.12–35.88. refineR returned 35.35–49.09, close to the disease population’s interval, while anchoredRI returned 24.23–35.96. This breaks refineR’s assumption that most patients meet the reference criteria and illustrates the value of screened patients.

Heavy overlap is a different problem. Across 20 shifted-lognormal datasets with heavy overlap and 50% of patients with disease, anchoredRI’s mean error was 51.4%, compared with 18.0% for the same 40 screened patients alone. Its single matched example had only 2.3% error. That discrepancy is why the repeated results and single examples are kept separate.

The new pooled-mixture comparison isolates service grouping within this design: services differ by only ten percentage points in the share of patients with disease, with shared component distributions. Its small overall benefit here does not settle performance with larger service differences. The estimator was frozen and was not tuned to these results. This follow-up reuses the original simulation grid; independent validation, confidence-interval coverage and clinical data remain necessary.
